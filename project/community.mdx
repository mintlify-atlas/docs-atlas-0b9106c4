EVerest is an open-source project under the [Linux Foundation Energy](https://lfenergy.org/) umbrella, developed by a global community of engineers, companies, and enthusiasts who share a common goal: an open, modular, and interoperable firmware stack for EV charging infrastructure. Whether you are integrating EVerest into a commercial product, researching EV protocols, or simply curious about the technology, the community welcomes your participation.

## Live Meetings and Working Groups

The community meets regularly through video calls that are open to everyone. Dial-in links and the full schedule are published in the [official EVerest event calendar](https://zoom-lfx.platform.linuxfoundation.org/meetings/everest?view=week).

### General EVerest Welcome Call

The General EVerest Welcome Call is a weekly, non-technical meeting held every **Tuesday at 11:00 CET/CEST**. It is the best entry point for newcomers who want to understand how the community is organized, ask questions about their specific use case, or find out which working group is the right fit for them.

## Technical Steering Committee

The Technical Steering Committee (TSC) is the governing body of the EVerest project. It is responsible for overall project health and direction, coordination of activities, resolution of technical disputes, and working with other projects and committees within Linux Foundation Energy.

TSC meetings are open to the community. You can find the current TSC members, meeting minutes, and participation instructions in the [TSC documentation](https://everest.github.io/nightly/project/governance/tsc/).

**TSC responsibilities include:**
- Setting the overall technical roadmap
- Approving new committers and maintainers
- Defining and updating governance policies
- Coordinating major releases and cross-cutting initiatives
- Representing EVerest within the broader LF Energy ecosystem

## Governance Model

EVerest is governed in a transparent and accessible manner. Participation is open and not bound to any corporate affiliation. The project follows a meritocratic ladder with three contributor roles:

| Role | Description |
|---|---|
| **Contributor** | Anyone who submits code, documentation, or other content to the project. |
| **Committer** | Has write access to repositories; reviews and merges pull requests after approval; actively triages issues. |
| **Maintainer** | A committer with defined code ownership (listed in `.github/CODEOWNERS`); automatically requested as reviewer for their areas. |

Advancement through these roles is merit-based and governed by the TSC. The full governance policy, including the voting process and conflict-resolution procedure, is documented in the [EVerest Governance Policy](https://everest.github.io/nightly/project/governance/governance.html). The formal technical charter is available from the [LF Energy foundation repository](https://github.com/lf-energy/foundation/blob/main/project_charters/everest_charter.pdf).

All community members are bound by the [Linux Foundation Projects Code of Conduct](https://lfprojects.org/policies/code-of-conduct/).

## Security Policy

EVerest takes security seriously. If you discover a vulnerability, please **do not** file a public GitHub issue. Instead, use one of the following private reporting paths:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Security Mailing List" icon="shield-halved">
    Send details to **everest-security@lists.lfenergy.org**. The team monitors this list on working days and aims to respond within 7 days.
  </Card>
  <Card title="GitHub Private Reporting" icon="github" href="https://github.com/EVerest/EVerest/security">
    Use the **"Report a vulnerability"** feature on GitHub, which is enabled across all EVerest repositories.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

When reporting, please include your severity assessment and as many technical details as you can provide — reproduction steps, affected versions, and potential exploit vectors all help the team respond faster.

The [EVerest Security Policy](https://everest.github.io/nightly/project/governance/security.html) describes the full process, including supported version windows for security backports.

## LF Energy EV Charging Special Interest Group

Beyond EVerest itself, Linux Foundation Energy hosts an EV Charging Special Interest Group (SIG) that brings together participants from multiple LFE projects. The SIG focuses on exchanging experience, sharing best practices, discussing industry trends, and tackling cross-project challenges.

Participation is free and open to anyone working in the EV charging space, whether or not they contribute to EVerest directly.

- **Mailing list:** [ev-charging-sig on LF Energy lists](https://lists.lfenergy.org/g/ev-charging-sig)
- **Meeting calendar:** [EV Charging SIG Zoom](https://zoom-lfx.platform.linuxfoundation.org/meetings/ev-charging-sig)

## Roadmap

The EVerest roadmap is driven by the community and updated through formal alignment rounds held one to two times per year. Between rounds, working groups continue to refine near-term priorities. The roadmap is a statement of current intent, not a delivery commitment — it moves as contributors, priorities, and standards evolve.

To see what is planned or to contribute your own input, join the relevant working group or review the published [EVerest Roadmap](https://everest.github.io/nightly/project/roadmap.html).
